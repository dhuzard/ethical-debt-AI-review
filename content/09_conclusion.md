# Conclusion: Repaying the Ethical Debt

{ref}`sec-introduction` opened with a promise and a ledger: animals are used on the understanding that the knowledge obtained will be worth their welfare cost, and the Ethical Debt is what accrues when that knowledge is never delivered. The intervening sections have shown that the debt is not a rhetorical device but a measurable quantity, visible at every stage of the loss. The knowledge itself is compromised — the published animal record is filtered toward positive results, weakly protected against bias, and a poor, analysis-dependent predictor of human outcomes {cite:p}`Sena2010, Freedman2015`, with a direct six-intervention comparison showing benefit in animal models that vanished in patients and clinical success from first-in-human testing running at only single-digit-to-low-double-digit percentages {cite:p}`Perel2006, Schuhmacher2025`. Whole classes of intervention illustrate the attrition, most starkly the hundreds of agents reported effective in animal models of Alzheimer's disease with essentially none translating {cite:p}`Shineman2011`. Even where the animal data are not in dispute, a defensible change in analysis can flip a model from mimicking human disease to failing to, so that translatability is decided by analytic choices as much as by biology {cite:p}`Seok2013, Takao2014`. The data that would let this record be checked or reused are largely absent — only about half of animal experiments are ever published, and only around 2% of medical papers actually share their data against the roughly 8% that declare they do {cite:p}`terRiet2012, Hamilton2023`. And the welfare cost is paid regardless: a retrospective harm–benefit analysis judged fewer than 7% of preclinical studies ethically permissible on their realised balance of harm and benefit, with analgesia unreported in the overwhelming majority {cite:p}`Pound2018, Bara2014`. The debt is real, it is quantifiable, and its unit of account is animal lives.

It is also self-reinforcing, which is why {ref}`sec-data-welfare` insisted on treating it as a welfare problem rather than a filing problem. The reproducibility failures catalogued in {ref}`sec-repro-crisis` and the stewardship failures catalogued thereafter are not two problems but one loop. Because Reduction was always a claim about information — use no more animals than the knowledge requires — a dataset that cannot be found, trusted, or reused is knowledge that must be regenerated, and the only way to regenerate knowledge from live tissue is to use more animals {cite:p}`Kramer2015, Verderio2023`. Unusable data are therefore not a downstream inconvenience but a driver of new animal use, and the loop tightens itself: the same reward structure that discourages complete reporting also produces the underpowered, unblinded studies whose results will not replicate, so the field runs experiments it cannot trust and then cannot reuse the animals' data to run better ones {cite:p}`Smaldino2016, Freedman2015`. The harm is doubled at the bench, because the animals bred to regenerate lost data are disproportionately animals killed without ever entering an analysis, and euthanising surplus animals is among the strongest reported stressors on the people who must do it {cite:p}`Persson2024, LaFollette2020`. Naming this loop is the review's central conceptual contribution, because it converts an abstract data-management concern into a specific, actionable claim about the 3Rs, and it locates the harm precisely where the field is least inclined to look — in the byte that is discarded rather than the procedure that is performed {cite:p}`Pound2018, Hamilton2023`.

The four repayment mechanisms are best understood as interventions that interrupt the loop at different points, and the body of the review has weighed each one honestly rather than advocating for it. FAIR stewardship interrupts the loop where data are lost to unfindability and non-reuse, but {ref}`sec-fair-preclinical` showed the generic FAIR ecosystem is declared far more than it is delivered: formal data-management compliance coexists with near-total non-sharing, even shared data score poorly on reusability, and realized reuse depends less on the principles than on whether a particular repository enforces and checks a standard at deposit {cite:p}`Wilkinson2016, VanTuyl2016, Markiewicz2021`. Virtual control groups interrupt the loop at the most concrete point available, reusing curated control-animal data so that a group of animals never enters the next study; {ref}`sec-virtual-controls` found a stable Reduction estimate of roughly a quarter of control animals and an infrastructure that already exists at scale, but a dividend conditional on endpoint-specific concordance and still unaccepted by any regulator for a pivotal preclinical study {cite:p}`StegerHartmann2020, Gurjanov2024b, DuchateauNguyen2026, SATO2024`. New approach methodologies interrupt the loop by removing the animal altogether, and {ref}`sec-nams-data` documented real, sometimes high, predictivity for narrow endpoints — yet also that Replacement pursued without data welfare simply relocates the debt into high-dimensional non-animal datasets that accumulate faster than they are curated and that suffer the same variable readouts as the animal models they replace {cite:p}`Comess2020, Carmichael2022, Hartung2025a, Kato2022`. The reuse of control data is itself double-edged, moreover: the same historical database can be marshalled to dismiss a marginal tumour finding as within the historical range or to support it as exceeding that range, a reminder that reuse without disciplined, outlier-robust method can mislead as readily as it can save animals {cite:p}`Panzacchi2025, Aguilar2017`. Each mechanism is a genuine lever; none is self-executing, and each inherits, in a new medium, the immature FAIR adoption that afflicts the animal data {cite:p}`Inau2023`.

What they share is a single precondition, and identifying it is the argumentative pivot of the review. The stewardship failure recurs across FAIR data, virtual controls, and non-animal methods not because each field is separately deficient but because they answer to the same reward structure — one that pays for publication counts and journal prestige while pricing data stewardship at essentially zero and treating a researcher's data as a private asset with no release incentive {cite:p}`Rice2020, McKiernan2019, Fecher2015`. {ref}`sec-incentives` framed this as a political economy in which non-sharing, selective reporting, and — at the margin — outright fabrication are individually rational responses to the system that rewards them, so that questionable practices are widely admitted and fraud-enabling operations are growing faster than legitimate science {cite:p}`Smaldino2016, Schneider2024, Richardson2025`. This is why voluntary virtue has failed to move the field for a generation, and why an editorial mandate to declare data availability produced near-universal declarations but under one percent of trials with usable public data {cite:p}`Danchev2021`. It follows that the four mechanisms break the loop only if incentive and governance reform makes stewardship count. This is not a hopeful addendum to the technical mechanisms; it is their enabling condition, because a virtual control group that no committee will accept, a FAIR mandate that no one checks, and a NAM dataset that earns its author no credit are all instances of the same inert policy {cite:p}`SATO2024, Hamilton2022, Gant2026`. Even the reform instruments are contested at the root, the field split over whether quantitative metrics can be re-engineered into a force for good or are inherently corrupting and should be abandoned rather than repaired {cite:p}`Ioannidis2023b, Gadd2021`.

The governance evidence gives that pivot its one firm foothold. {ref}`sec-governance` showed that the heterogeneous, often-null effects of reporting guidelines and sharing policies resolve into a pattern once they are sorted by whether compliance was merely endorsed or actually enforced: a checklist requested at submission changed almost nothing, while the same class of checklist mandated and checked at revision moved reporting where matched control journals did not, and verified open-practice signals delivered usable data where unenforced mandates did not {cite:p}`Hair2019, Macleod2019b, Kidwell2016`. A mandatory, checked data-and-code policy pushed provision to nearly all manuscripts, confirming that the operative variable is verification rather than the requirement itself {cite:p}`IvimeyCook2025`. The levers are not scarce — hundreds of reporting guidelines already exist, and ARRIVE was even reorganised into an "Essential 10" after a decade of endorsement failed to lift reporting — so the binding constraint is enforcement, not the absence of a standard {cite:p}`Christensen2026, PercieduSert2020a`. The clinical-trial registries tell the same story at scale — a publication condition drove registrations fivefold, yet results reporting decayed wherever it was left unaudited and rose again wherever institutions were monitored and named {cite:p}`Viergever2015, Goldacre2018`. But the foothold is narrow. Ethical-review bodies, the lever closest to the animal, mostly lack the expertise and mandate to judge scientific merit or to check that a study is non-duplicative {cite:p}`Pippin2025`, effects are heterogeneous and frequently small, and field-wide sharing remains near the ~2% floor even as concentrated local stewardship at a single, actively managed institution can lift it severalfold without generalising {cite:p}`Hamilton2023, Deeb2025, Hamilton2022`. The lesson is directional, not triumphant: the field knows which lever works and has barely begun to pull it.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions and should temper any reading of them as settled. The most important is that the review's headline magnitudes are uneven in provenance. The most-repeated figures — the modelled tens-of-billions irreproducibility cost, the "most research is wasted" synthesis, even the specific industry-reproducibility percentages — rest on modelled or non-transparent inputs and are best read as order-of-magnitude claims rather than measurements, a caution we have flagged wherever they appear {cite:p}`Freedman2015, Ioannidis2014c`. A minority position holds the crisis narrative is itself overstated and better described as methodological empowerment, and although the balance of evidence weighs against it, the dispute is genuinely open and we have not treated the crisis as more severe than the data support {cite:p}`Fanelli2018, Sena2010`. Because the same phenomenon yields very different values by method, we have preferred juxtaposition of divergent estimates to single anchors, and the review's own quantitative claims inherit that method-dependence {cite:p}`terRiet2012, Deutsch2021`. A second limitation is conceptual: the data-welfare construct is load-bearing only where lost data could genuinely have been banked and reused, and merely rhetorical where reuse was impossible in principle; treating every unshared file as literally equivalent to animal suffering would trivialise the physical harms the 3Rs were built to address, and the honest version of the argument concedes this boundary {cite:p}`Pound2018, Kramer2015`. A third is scope: the ~2% availability anchor is a field-wide medical estimate, and even the prescription that would exploit reuse — whether to standardise or to heterogenise animal studies — remains unresolved, so the operational advice the review can give is bounded {cite:p}`Hamilton2023, Voelkl2018, Richter2011`.

The open empirical questions are correspondingly specific, and each one names a place where the repayment argument outruns the evidence. First, it remains unproven that FAIRification *causally* increases reuse rather than merely raising nominal availability: optimistic reviews and pessimistic retrieval audits disagree {cite:p}`Inau2023, VanTuyl2016`, and even the direction of the effect is uncertain because automated FAIRification can inject the very deficiencies it is meant to cure {cite:p}`Tahar2026`. Second, it is not yet known whether regulators will accept virtual control groups, and the endpoint-dependence of their concordance together with the temporal and cross-laboratory drift of historical data mean the answer may differ for categorical toxicity calls and for continuous clinical-pathology parameters {cite:p}`DuchateauNguyen2026, Adedeji2024, Kellner2025, SATO2024`. The clinical cousin of the virtual control — the external control arm — already features in a meaningful share of oncology approvals, which suggests the preclinical acceptance gap is a matter of validation and precedent rather than of principle {cite:p}`Pignatti2026, Harrell2024`. Third, and most consequential, it is unproven that evaluation reform changes behaviour rather than metrics: the reform manifesto and the natural-selection model make opposite predictions, and a scoping review found that of more than a hundred studies of reproducibility interventions only a handful measured reproducibility itself rather than a presumed proxy {cite:p}`Munafo2017, Smaldino2016, Dudda2025`. Underlying all three is a single unmeasured link: the causal chain from a governance requirement, through actual data reuse, to fewer animals used has essentially never been quantified, so the review's central Reduction claim rests on a projection rather than a measurement, and building the infrastructure to measure it — standardised, FAIR control-animal data whose reuse can be tracked to an animal-count saving — is itself a research priority {cite:p}`PetitDemouliere2026, Wright2023b, Deeb2025`.

None of these caveats overturns the argument; they locate its frontier. The debt is real, measurable, and self-reinforcing, and it is repayable — FAIR stewardship, data reuse and virtual control groups, and disciplined stewardship of the data that non-animal methods generate each break the loop, provided incentive and governance reform makes stewardship count {cite:p}`Wilkinson2016, StegerHartmann2020, Comess2020, Macleod2019b`. Where stewardship has been made to count — a checklist gated at revision, an open-data signal that is verified — behaviour has moved and usable data have followed, so the direction of travel is not in doubt even if its pace is {cite:p}`Kidwell2016, IvimeyCook2025`. The strongest claim of this review is also its simplest, and it survives every qualification above: because animals have already paid for the data with their welfare and, usually, their lives, letting the data go to waste is a welfare harm in its own right, and repaying the debt saves animals {cite:p}`Pound2018, Kramer2015, Hamilton2023`. That is the promise {ref}`sec-introduction` began with, restated at the end not as a new duty invented here but as an obligation the field already holds under the 3Rs {cite:p}`Strech2019, Verderio2023`. The knowledge extracted from a sentient life should be worth what the life cost — and it should be preserved, shared, and reused as if it were.
